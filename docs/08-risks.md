# 8. Biggest Risks

Ranked by **likelihood × impact** for a new Miami dog daycare (Estimate).

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Main ways to reduce it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dog fights, bites and injuries** (to dogs or staff) | High | High | Temperament tests, size and energy grouping, 1:10–15 ratios, trained staff, incident SOPs, waivers, care-custody-control insurance |
| 2 | **Contagious disease outbreak** | Medium | High | Vaccine policy, daily health checks, isolation room, disinfection protocol, temporary closure plan |
| 3 | **Cash shortfall during the ramp-up** | Medium–High | High | 6 months' working capital, waitlist before opening, memberships, phased hiring |
| 4 | **Zoning, permitting or build-out delays and overruns** | High | Medium–High | Lease contingent on approvals, rent abatement, early zoning determination, 15–20% contingency |
| 5 | **Hurricanes, flooding, power loss** | Medium (every season) | High | Generator, evacuation and closure plan, flood-zone site check, insurance (wind, flood, business interruption) |
| 6 | **Heat stress / heatstroke** | Medium | High | Indoor AC play (county code requires 50–85°F indoors), limits on outdoor time, shaded and cool turf, breed-specific rules (brachycephalic breeds) |
| 7 | **Labour cost and staff turnover** | High | Medium | Pay above minimum, career ladder, scheduling software, owner on site |
| 8 | **Competition and price pressure** | Medium | Medium | Differentiated positioning, location, reviews, memberships |
| 9 | **Noise and odour complaints from neighbours** | Medium | Medium | Site selection away from homes, sound insulation, waste management |
| 10 | **Regulatory change** (county licensing recently removed; rules could return or tighten) | Low–Medium | Medium | Monitor state and county legislation; operate to the Chapter 5 standards regardless |
| 11 | **Key-person dependence** (owner, lead handler) | Medium | Medium | SOP documentation, cross-training |
| 12 | **Reputation damage from one viral incident** | Low–Medium | High | Transparent incident communication, crisis plan, strong review base |

## Detail on the top risks

### 1. Injuries and liability
- Group play always carries some risk of scuffles. Serious fights, escapes and deaths are rare but can be catastrophic for the business.
- Florida dog owners are strictly liable for bites (F.S. 767.04). While dogs are in its care, the daycare is a bailee (temporary custodian) and can face claims for vet bills and damages.
- **General liability usually does NOT cover injury to dogs in your care.** That needs **care, custody and control (animal bailee)** coverage. Sample CCC limits can be as low as ~$5,000 per year. **Buy higher limits.**
- Insurance set to quote: general liability, CCC/bailee, property (wind and flood), business interruption, workers' comp, umbrella, commercial auto (if transporting dogs), employment practices.

### 2. Disease
- **Miami-Dade precedent:** in 2022, Animal Services warned about a Canine Infectious Respiratory Disease Complex (CIRDC) outbreak later identified as **canine pneumovirus**, and **advised owners to avoid dog parks and doggie daycares**. During outbreaks, demand can drop sharply.
- **Leptospirosis:** in the 2021 Los Angeles outbreak, about 66% of cases had exposure at a daycare or boarding facility (201 cases, 13 deaths). South Florida's warm, wet climate favours leptospirosis all year round (CBS Miami). Require or strongly recommend the lepto vaccine, and avoid standing water and wildlife access in outdoor yards.
- **Canine influenza H3N2:** public health guidance recommends vaccination before daycare.
- Chapter 5 already prohibits accepting dogs with known or suspected communicable disease.

### 3. Financial
- The model in [04-costs.md](04-costs.md) shows break-even at **about 55 dogs a day** at a 6,000 sq ft site, with labour at about 50% of revenue. The margin for error is small, so add boarding and grooming and keep tight control of labour.
- APPA data shows owners becoming more value-focused (22% spent less in 2025).

### 4. Real estate and permitting
- Industrial vacancy is low (~6%), so suitable space is scarce. Landlords may resist dog uses (noise, wear and tear, drainage).
- A change of use can trigger fire sprinkler, ADA and sewer upgrades that cost far more than expected.

### 5–6. Climate
- Atlantic hurricane season runs **1 June–30 November**. Plan for closure days, client communication, generator power for AC and refrigeration, and a policy for boarders' dogs during evacuations.
- Many Miami areas are in FEMA flood zones. Check the flood zone and elevation of any site.
- Summer heat index regularly exceeds 100°F. Artificial turf can get dangerously hot, so schedule outdoor play early or late, and provide shade and misting.

## Go / no-go triggers for the Strategy agent (Estimate)
- **Go** if: a site zoned for the use is secured at ≤ $25/sq ft all-in, the build-out is ≤ $90/sq ft after the TI allowance, there are ≥ 150 waitlist sign-ups, and funding covers the build-out plus 6 months of working capital.
- **No-go / rethink** if: zoning requires a contested public hearing, the all-in cost exceeds $1M for an independent site, or the waitlist test does not support a price of ≥ $38/day.
