# 1. Market Overview

## National picture

| Metric | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Total US pet industry spending, 2025 | **$158B** (+3.7% vs 2024) | Verified (APPA, 2026 report) |
| Projected 2026 spending | **$165B** | Verified (APPA forecast) |
| US households owning a dog, 2025 | **53% (~71M households)**, up from 51% in 2024 | Verified (APPA) |
| Average annual spend per dog | **> $2,400** (about double the ~$1,200 of 2015) | Verified (APPA) |
| "Other services" spend (boarding, grooming, training, sitting, walking, insurance) | **$14.3B** | Verified (APPA). Daycare is not reported on its own. |

**Trends worth noting (APPA 2025–26):**
- Owners are becoming **value-conscious**. 22% said they spent less on their pets in 2025. Spending on boarding and grooming fell slightly.
- **Use of kennels and boarding is declining.** About 4 in 10 owners have a friend or family member care for the pet at home when they travel. This is pressure on overnight boarding. *Daytime* care for working owners is a different need.
- Younger owners (Millennials and Gen Z) treat dogs as family. They pay for enrichment, socialisation and convenience, which helps premium daycare.
- Hybrid and return-to-office work patterns create demand for care on specific weekdays rather than every day. *Estimate:* plan for 2–3 day/week memberships as the core product.

## Miami-Dade picture

| Metric | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Miami-Dade population | ~2.80M (July 2025 estimate), +3.7% since 2020 | Verified (Census QuickFacts) |
| Households | ~975,000 | Verified (Census, 2020–24 ACS) |
| Median household income | ~$72k (ACS 2020–24); ~$76k (SAIPE 2024) | Verified (Census / FRED) |
| Poverty rate | 14.2% | Verified (Census) |
| Metro households owning a dog (Miami–Ft Lauderdale–West Palm Beach) | **~23% (~507,500 households)** | Verified (Census American Housing Survey 2021, via Axios) |
| Metro overall pet ownership rate | ~29% vs ~50% nationally (lowest of 15 large metros), but **3rd for absolute number of dog owners** | Reported (Stacker / Local10) |
| Dogs licensed in Miami-Dade (FY2024-25) | **226,334** (target 235,000) | Verified (Miami-Dade Animal Services Q4 report) |

### What this means

- Miami-Dade has **fewer pet owners per household than most US metros**, likely because of a large renter population, small apartments and pet restrictions in condos. In absolute terms the number of dogs is still large.
- *Estimate:* if roughly 20–23% of Miami-Dade's ~975k households have a dog, that is **~195k–225k dog-owning households**. This matches the ~226k licensed dogs. Licensing understates the true number.
- **Serviceable market (Estimate):** daycare is used mostly by higher-income urban owners with medium or large, sociable dogs. Assume 5–10% of dog-owning households within a 15–20 minute drive of a site use daycare at least occasionally. That gives a site in a dense core (e.g. Brickell / Downtown / Edgewater) a catchment of a few thousand potential clients. A facility needs only about **150–300 active clients** to reach 50–70 dogs a day, so demand at a well-chosen site is sufficient. Winning customers from competitors is the harder problem.
- **Housing:** Miami REALTORS data (2024) shows pet-friendly rentals rent for more and lease faster. New luxury rental and condo towers in Brickell, Edgewater, Wynwood and Doral are often pet-friendly and attract young professionals who own dogs.

## Demand drivers

1. **Dense high-rise living**: little yard space and small units, so dogs need exercise during the day.
2. **Long working hours and commutes**, plus growth in finance, tech and professional services (Brickell, Downtown).
3. **Tourism, seasonal residents and travel**: demand for boarding from snowbirds and frequent flyers (if boarding is offered).
4. **Climate**: heat and storms for much of the year make long walks hard, which supports indoor, air-conditioned play.
5. **Pet humanisation and willingness to pay** for premium services (grooming, training, webcams).

## Demand headwinds

- Price sensitivity is rising (APPA: 22% spending less).
- Remote and hybrid workers can watch their dogs at home on remote days.
- Low-cost substitutes: Rover and Wag sitters, dog walkers, friends and family.
- Overall pet ownership in Miami is lower than in most metros.

## Open questions
- Get Miami-Dade-only (not metro) dog-ownership data at ZIP code level. Options: the Census AHS microdata, or a request to Miami-Dade Animal Services for license counts by ZIP.
- Run a site-specific catchment analysis: households, income, renter share and new multifamily deliveries within a 3- and 5-mile radius of each shortlisted site.
- Look for a paid industry report (e.g. IBISWorld "Pet Grooming & Boarding") that gives daycare-specific market size.
