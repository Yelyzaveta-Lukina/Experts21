# 6. Staffing

## Staff-to-dog ratios

There is **no US federal or Florida-mandated ratio** for dog daycare. Benchmarks found:

| Source | Ratio | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Industry commentary / risk management | **1:10 to 1:15** in active group play; **1:10** is described as the "insurance-friendly" figure | Reported |
| US industry capstone study | Recommended 1:15. Some facilities stretch to 1:30 (unsafe). | Reported (weak) |
| Winnipeg (Canada) regulation | 1:20 large dogs, 1:25 small dogs | Verified (foreign rule) |
| UK local authority guidance | Normally ≤ 1:10 | Verified (foreign rule) |
| Iowa (US) rule | At least 1 person assigned to every playgroup, continuous supervision | Verified |

**Recommendation (Estimate):** set **1:10–1:12 for large or high-energy groups, up to 1:15 for calm or small-dog groups**, and **never leave a playgroup without a person present**. Publish the ratio as a trust signal. Check that insurers accept it.

### Wage data
- **BLS (May 2023):** animal caretakers in the Miami–Fort Lauderdale–West Palm Beach metro: **mean $17.45/hr ($36,300/yr)**, about 4,460 employed. National median $15.00/hr.
- **Florida minimum wage:** **$15.00/hr from 30 Sep 2026** (Amendment 2 schedule; adjusted for inflation each year after that). This is the floor for every hourly role.
- Salary.com: Miami "animal care attendant" ~$19.55/hr (Reported). Florida "kennel assistant" ~$13/hr (Reported, below today's minimum wage, so outdated).
- **Burden:** add ~12–18% for payroll taxes (FICA, FUTA, Florida reemployment tax), workers' comp (required with 4+ employees) and any benefits.

## Scheduling pattern (Estimate)
- Open 7:00am–7:00pm on weekdays. Dog counts peak from 9am to 4pm. Drop-off rush 7–9am, pickup rush 5–7pm.
- Use **staggered 8-hour shifts** (6:30–2:30, 9–5, 11–7:30) so the ratio holds at the peak while the edges of the day run lean.
- Weekends: reduced hours, or boarding and grooming only.
- Plan **~50 hourly staff-hours a day** for 55–65 dogs (see the cost model in [04-costs.md](04-costs.md)).

## Training and certification
- **Pet CPR & first aid** for every handler (e.g. Red Cross, PetTech).
- **Dog body language and playgroup management** (e.g. Dog Daycare Academy, Dogs Playing for Life, Pet Care Services Association/IBPSA certifications, PACCC credentials).
- **Fight-interruption protocol** (spray bottles, break sticks, slip leads, no hands between dogs), **incident reporting**, **heat-stress recognition** (very important in Miami), and **disinfection and disease protocols**.
- Onboarding: 1–2 weeks of shadowing before supervising a group alone.

## Hiring and retention
- Main talent pools: veterinary assistant programs (Miami Dade College, vocational schools), university students (UM, FIU), pet-industry workers, and bilingual (English/Spanish) candidates.
- Turnover in this industry is high (work is physical, pay is low, bites are a risk). Retention tools: pay above minimum wage, consistent schedules, free daycare for staff dogs, a training ladder (handler → lead → trainer), recognition, and small bonuses tied to safety and reviews.
- Background checks and reference checks; driver checks if offering pickup and drop-off.
