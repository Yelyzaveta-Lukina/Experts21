# 5. Permits, Licenses and Regulation

> **Disclaimer:** this is research context, not legal advice. Before signing a lease, the Legal & Compliance agent must confirm every item with the relevant agency, a Florida business attorney and a zoning or land-use consultant. The rules have **changed recently**, and some official pages contradict each other.

## Major recent change: county animal business licences

- Miami-Dade Animal Services' own service page now says that, **"in light of recent state legislation," it no longer requires licenses for breeders, hobby breeders, guard dog services, kennel operators, mobile groomers, pet care centers and pet dealers.** It continues to apply its **standards** to these businesses under **Chapter 5 of the County Code**. (Verified: county page. The specific state bill was **not identified** in this research.)
- A secondary source says this took effect **1 July 2025** and directs kennel questions to the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS). (Reported)
- Older county pages and the Municode text still describe annual pet care center and kennel licences. **Confirm the current position in writing with Animal Services before relying on it.**
- **No statewide licence for dog daycares or boarding kennels was found.** A 2025 Leon County news report says Florida has no state requirement for boarding kennels to register beyond an occupational licence and zoning. (Reported)

## Miami-Dade Chapter 5 operating standards (still apply)

The Miami-Dade Code defines a **"pet care center"** as an establishment **operating during daytime hours only** that provides supervised care for cats or dogs **in an air-conditioned indoor facility**, and which does not breed. Requirements found in the code text include:

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Indoor temperature | Keep between **50°F and 85°F** |
| Sick animals | **May not accept** any animal with a known or suspected communicable or infectious disease. Animals under treatment must be housed according to accepted veterinary practice. |
| Cleaning | Clean each primary enclosure **after each animal leaves for the day** |
| Emergency care | Get a **medical emergency release form** from each owner |
| Signage | Sec. 5-17.7 sets signage requirements for kennels, groomers and commercial animal establishments (originally about pit bulls; Miami-Dade's pit bull ban was repealed in 2023. Check whether this section is still in force.) |

**Important classification point:** "daytime only" fits a **pet care center**. Overnight boarding is usually classified as a **kennel**, which may bring different zoning and standards. Decide the service mix before applying for zoning.

## Dog-level requirements (passed to clients)
- **Rabies vaccination** is required by Florida law for dogs aged 4 months and older (F.S. 828.30).
- **Miami-Dade dog licence tag** is required for every dog older than 4 months, tied to rabies vaccination. Check it at intake.
- Industry-standard intake requirements (set by policy, not law): rabies, DHPP/DAPP, **Bordetella** and often **canine influenza (H3N2/H3N8)** and **leptospirosis** (both recommended by public health officials after daycare outbreaks), negative fecal test, flea and tick prevention, spay/neuter by ~6–7 months, and a temperament assessment.

## Zoning notes

- **City of Miami (Miami 21 code):** find the parcel's Transect Zone on MuniMap, then check **Article 4, Table 3** for whether the use is allowed. "R" means allowed by right, "W" by Warrant (administrative), "E" by Exception (public hearing), and blank means prohibited. Dog daycare may be classed under an animal-related or commercial use, so ask Planning for a **written Determination of Use** before signing a lease.
- Industrial and light-industrial zones (e.g. D1/D2 in Miami 21; IU-1 in the county) or commercial zones are the most likely fits. Residential adjacency brings **noise (barking) and odour** objections.
- **Noise:** Miami-Dade and city noise ordinances apply. Barking is the most common neighbour complaint, so design in sound control and keep outdoor play away from homes.
- Each municipality (Doral, Coral Gables, Miami Beach, North Miami, Aventura and others) has its own code and definition of the use. **North Miami**, for example, defines a dog daycare as a daytime-only supervised care establishment.
- **Leases:** make the lease **contingent on zoning approval and permits**, with a rent-free period during the build-out.

## Contracts and waivers (best practice)
- Client service agreement and liability waiver: accept the risks of group play, authorise emergency veterinary treatment (also required by Chapter 5), payment authorisation, vaccine attestation, and the right to refuse or remove a dog.
- Florida law makes **dog owners strictly liable for bites** (F.S. 767.04). A daycare acting as "bailee" (temporary custodian) can also be liable for injuries to dogs in its care. **Have a Florida attorney draft the waiver.**

## Open questions
- Ask Miami-Dade Animal Services: (a) which state law removed the licences, and when; (b) which Chapter 5 standards are still enforced, and how (inspections, complaints).
- Ask FDACS Division of Animal Industry whether any state registration applies to daycare or boarding.
- Get a written zoning use determination for each shortlisted parcel.
- Confirm with FDOR whether pet daycare and boarding services are subject to sales tax.
- Find the current BTR fee category and amount for "pet care center / kennel" from the City of Miami and the county.
