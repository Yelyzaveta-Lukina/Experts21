# Miami Doggy Day Care: Research Pack

Background research for opening a dog day care in Miami, Florida (Miami-Dade County). It is the shared context for the team of expert agents that will be built on top of this repo. Each agent should read this index first and then the topic files relevant to its role.

Research date: **November 2026**. Figures come from public web sources: government sites, industry associations, commercial real estate reports, franchise disclosures and vendor blogs. Each file lists its sources.

## How to read these docs

Every claim is labelled with one of three levels of confidence:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | Taken from a primary or official source (Census, BLS, county code, APPA, franchise disclosure). |
| **Reported** | From a secondary source (news, directories, vendor or industry blogs). Treat it as directional. |
| **Estimate** | Our own working assumption or model, built from the figures above. Replace it with real quotes before making a decision. |

Each file ends with **Open questions**: things that need a phone call, a site visit or a professional (attorney, CPA, broker, insurance agent) before anyone commits money.

## Summary of findings

1. **Demand is real but not unlimited.** About 23% of households in the Miami metro own a dog (2021 Census data), compared with roughly half nationally. Miami ranks low for pet ownership overall but high for the number of dog owners. Miami-Dade licensed about 226,000 dogs in FY2024-25. Demand concentrates in dense, high-income, apartment-heavy areas where people work long hours: Brickell, Downtown, Edgewater/Wynwood, Midtown, Coral Gables, Doral and Aventura.
2. **Prices are mid-market.** A full day of daycare costs about **$30–$50 per day** in Miami. Rover's average for Miami-based sitters is about $38/day (September 2025). Boutique facilities charge $35–$45+. Packages and memberships give regular customers a discount.
3. **The market is competitive and fragmented.** Competitors include independents (Dogtown, Doggie Dojo, Bark Square, Doggies Gone Wild, Woof N Wag), local multi-site brands (D.O.G. has four resorts), national franchises, and sitter apps (Rover, Wag). To win, you need a strong location, convenience (long hours, parking) and a reputation for safety.
4. **Real estate is the largest cost.** Miami industrial and flex space rents for about **$17–$22+/sq ft per year NNN**. Converting a space for dogs (drains, HVAC, sound control, special flooring) is expensive. Our estimate to open an independent site is **~$165k–$370k** for a lean 3,000 sq ft site in second-generation space, and **~$525k–$1.2M** for a 6,000 sq ft flex conversion. A Dogtopia franchise discloses **$600k–$1.4M+**. At Miami prices, daycare alone breaks even at about **55 dogs a day**. Boarding and grooming provide most of the margin.
5. **Licensing is unusually light, but zoning is hard.** Miami-Dade Animal Services says that, after recent state legislation, it **no longer issues pet care center or kennel licenses**. It still enforces Chapter 5 care standards: indoor temperature between 50 and 85°F, no sick animals accepted, emergency medical release forms. The real obstacles are **zoning approval, the Certificate of Use, building permits, and the city and county business tax receipts**.
6. **Labour is the largest ongoing cost.** Florida's minimum wage is **$15/hr from 30 Sep 2026**. Animal caretakers in the Miami metro averaged $17.45/hr (BLS, May 2023). Industry norm is about **1 handler per 10–15 dogs** in group play.
7. **The biggest risks** are dog fights and injuries (and the liability and reputation damage that follow), contagious disease (Miami-Dade had a canine pneumovirus outbreak in 2022 and the county advised owners to avoid daycares), hurricanes and extreme heat, being unable to find a property zoned for the use, and labour cost and staff turnover.

## Suggested agent roster (for the next phase)

> Superseded by the fuller roster in [expert-roster.md](expert-roster.md). The short list below is kept for reference.

- **Strategy / CEO agent**: decides go/no-go, location and positioning (reads everything).
- **Finance agent**: builds the startup budget, P&L and break-even model (01, 04, 06).
- **Legal & compliance agent**: entity setup, zoning, permits, contracts and waivers (05, 08).
- **Real estate agent**: site selection and lease negotiation (01, 02, 04, 05).
- **Operations agent**: facility layout, SOPs, intake and temperament testing, health protocols (05, 06, 08).
- **HR agent**: hiring, training and retention (06).
- **Marketing agent**: brand, pricing, launch plan and customer retention (02, 03, 07).
