# Survey & demand validation

## Methods
1. **Owner survey** (Google Forms / Typeform, English and Spanish). Aim for 200+ responses from target ZIP codes.
   - Screeners: owns a dog, ZIP code, housing type, work pattern (office/hybrid/remote and which days).
   - Current solution and spend (daycare, Rover, walker, nothing).
   - Needs: days per week, drop-off and pickup times, services wanted (boarding, grooming, training, transport).
   - Willingness to pay: Van Westendorp's four questions (too cheap / bargain / getting expensive / too expensive) **or** a 3-price choice ($35 / $42 / $50 per day).
   - Purchase drivers ranked: safety, location, hours, price, webcams, staff, outdoor space.
2. **Waitlist / founding-member landing page** with a real offer. Measure visit → sign-up conversion and deposits if possible.
3. **Pop-ups** at dog parks and pet-friendly buildings to collect leads in person.

## Thresholds (Estimate, `docs/08-risks.md`)
- **Go** signal: ≥ 150 waitlist sign-ups within the target catchment, and the price test supports ≥ $38/day realised.
- Survey: ≥ 30% of qualified respondents "very likely" to use daycare at the target price.

## Bias checks
Stated intent overstates real buying (discount it by ~50–70%). Social-media samples skew toward engaged owners. Check that the ZIP mix matches the catchment.

## Output
The instrument (questions), sampling plan, the results table, the conversion from intent to an expected number of clients, and what the result means for the go/no-go decision.
