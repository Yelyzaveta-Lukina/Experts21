# Pricing & package design

## Miami benchmarks (`docs/03-competitors.md`)
| Tier | $/day |
|---|---|
| Budget / older listings | $25–30 |
| Rover sitters | $29–50 (avg ~$38, Sept 2025) |
| Boutique (Dogtown from $35; Doggie Dojo $45 single) | $35–45 |
| Premium / franchise (Dogtopia-style) | $40–50+ |

## Starting price sheet (Estimate, `docs/07-marketing.md`)
Single day $45 · half day $32 · 10-pack $400 · 20-pack $760 · memberships 2/3/5 days per week ≈ $330 / $470 / $720 per month · 2nd dog −15% · bath $25–45 · nail trim $15 · enrichment $15–20 · transport $15–25 each way · boarding $60–75/night.

## Principles
1. **Make memberships the default.** They give recurring revenue, predictable staffing and lower churn.
2. **Guardrail:** the blended realised price per daycare day must stay ≥ $38 (the break-even assumption). Model the discount mix before launching any offer.
3. Price **convenience and safety**, not just time: published ratios, webcams, long hours.
4. Use add-ons to lift revenue per dog-day by 15%+.
5. Keep at most 3 or 4 headline products so the menu stays simple.
6. Founding-member offers: lock-in rates for a limited period (e.g. 6–12 months), not forever.

## Method
1. Define segments and their willingness to pay (`docs/02-target-customers.md`).
2. Draft the ladder (single → pack → membership) so each step saves 5–20%.
3. Compute the realised price under an assumed mix (e.g. 50% membership, 35% packs, 15% singles).
4. Check against the competitor ladder and the break-even model (hand off to the financial modeler).
5. Propose a test (waitlist offer or A/B landing page) before locking prices in.

## Output
A price table, the realised-price calculation, the competitive position, the risks (price war, membership cannibalisation) and the test plan.
